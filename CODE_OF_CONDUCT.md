# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be kind. Assume the other person is trying. This project exists for people who are having a hard
time, and that shapes what is welcome here.

## What is expected

- Assume good faith. Most disagreements are about approach, not about the other person's motives.
- Give feedback on the work. "This breaks the case where a block crosses midnight" is useful; "this
  is wrong" on its own is not.
- Respect the person behind the pull request. They are often a first-time open source contributor,
  and often someone who needed this app for themselves.
- Respect privacy. Do not post anyone's check-ins, notes, screenshots, or conversation with the coach.
  Use the synthetic personas in `dev-fixtures/` when you need an example.
- Take "no" for an answer, and take "not right now" the same way.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, slurs, or personal attacks, in issues, reviews, or discussions
- Sexualised language or imagery
- Dismissing someone's wellbeing, or pushing streak and guilt mechanics as a feature
- Medical advice, or presenting this project's output as medical advice
- Publishing anyone's private data without explicit permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces: the repository, issues, pull requests, discussions, and any
review of the code. It also applies when someone is representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainer through a private GitHub message, or privately through
[Security → Report a vulnerability](https://github.com/TheLinuxGuy-ssh/Mom.exe/security/advisories/new)
if it involves someone's privacy.

Reports are read and acted on. You will get a reply, and if something changes you will hear about
it. Reports are handled confidentially, and you will not be named publicly unless you ask to be.

## Enforcement

The maintainer is responsible for clarifying these standards and will do so fairly and reasonably
in the spirit of what was written here.

1. **Correction.** A private note explaining what was wrong and why. Most reports end here.
2. **Warning.** A warning with consequences stated. Continuing the behaviour may lead to a temporary
   ban.
3. **Temporary ban.** No interaction with the project for a defined period.
4. **Permanent ban.** For sustained behaviour, harassment, or a serious privacy violation.
